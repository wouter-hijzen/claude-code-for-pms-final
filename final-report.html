Rook Industries · Dispatch · course report (fictional scenario)

We shipped release 4.2.
Four responders stopped getting pings.

We released 4.2 on 12 August: a 60-second ping wait (was 90) and ranking weighted toward proximity. Tickets tripled, and four responders almost stopped getting pings. Most likely because a missed ping lowers ranking and nothing raises it again.

The plain version

What happened with release 4.2

4.2 shipped two changes at once: ranking leaned harder on proximity, and the ping wait dropped from 90 to 60 seconds. Underneath sits a scoring rule nobody had flagged.

29 Jun – 11 Aug · baseline
Acceptance flat at 75–78%. Missed pings 2–6 a week. Tickets 5–8 a week.
12 Aug · 4.2 ships
Proximity weight 0.45 → 0.60, acceptance weight 0.40 → 0.25, ping wait 90s → 60s. Clean rollout, no incident.
First two days
Farlight, Meteor Mite, The Undertow and Vesper are still pinged at the normal pace (14 pings), but 9 are missed. Cause of those early misses: still unexplained
Weeks of 17 Aug → 7 Sep
Pings to the four fall −26%, −75%, −88%, −96%. Handlers see "phone never goes off" tickets. Handlers can't see misses or ranking, so nobody connects it to the release.
14 Aug
Marcus Oyelaran (Engineering Manager) asks Wen Li whether the change was meant to hit responders who keep turning pings down. Unanswered. Priya Raghunathan's view: "seasonal, don't revert".
7 Sep · now
New PM has the data, the code, a one-pager and a prototype. Helen Achebe (Director of Product) asked for what to build, not a number change.

Six modules: one finding, one prompt each

3
RewindPer-responder data over time

Finding

The misses came first, then the pings dried up. In the first two days the four were pinged at the normal pace but 9 of 14 were missed; pings then fell by 26%, 75%, 88%, 96%. Pooled, the four went from 75.5% to 23.2% taken, with declines flat (22% → 18%). So this isn't people turning pings down.

Established Why the early misses? Unexplained

The prompt that got it

We need to understand the drop in pings and why the collapsed group missed so many pings. Miss rate before and after rose significantly for this group. Did these misses come before the reduction in pings, or did the pings reduce before the misses already?
4
X-Ray VisionReading the routing code

Finding

The proximity change is mostly ruled out. Before 4.2 Farlight was pinged on 59 of 59 Uptown callouts; after, 6 of 31, a slide over ~10 days rather than a step. In the code a miss costs the same as a decline (−0.12), the score never decays, and nothing targets decliners. That answered Marcus's 14 Aug question for the config half.

Code-verified Cause = hypothesis

The prompt that got it

Can we dig into the proximity change impact - does this have any impact before and after for Farlight specifically and if so, what is that?

The prompt that built it

I want to save a habit as something I can reuse. When I review a brief before it goes any further, here's what I actually check for: - It names who owns it - It says how we'll know it worked - The scope at the end matches the scope at the start - It explains the problem before it proposes a fix - It labels what's established versus a hypothesis versus proposed-not-built - It states the decision it needs, who makes it, and by when Turn that into a skill called review-checklist, so I can point it at any brief and get the same check every time, without me explaining it again.
The fix, through the prototype

Tell the handler, don't make them go looking

Helen asked what to build for quiet responders, not for another weight change. The prototype shows the handler the thing they currently can't see, and gives them a safe way to act.

1 · It flags the four

Rule: pings last week under 50% of the responder's own average while area demand holds. It catches Farlight, Meteor Mite, The Undertow and Vesper and nobody else.

2 · It shows scale

Pings per week, taken % against their usual, last missed pings, and how many of their area's callouts went to other areas.

3 · It offers real options

Check availability (exists today). Proposed Ask Rook to send the next area callout to them, a test ping, and "Tell Rook". Rook can say no and it's logged.

Try: pick "Handler for Farlight", then Request the next Uptown callout to go to Farlight → Send request → Approved/Declined. Switch to Responder phone to see the re-entry ping. If the frame is blank, open it directly.

Decisions made while building

  • Handler sees only their own responders; cross-area detail stays internal (Module 5, prompts 26 and 31).
  • A banner on the start screen, so nobody has to go and look. Meteor Mite's and Vesper's handlers filed no tickets (prompt 32).
  • Taken % added so the scale of the drop is visible, not just counts (prompt 32).
  • "Send a callout yourself" became "Request … to go to Farlight", so rules and audit apply and Rook can refuse (prompts 36–37).
  • "What happens next" removed as redundant (prompt 38).

Draft product answers not decided

  • Now: report missed pings in Ravi Menon's weekly report; stopgap score reset for the four.
  • Next release: a miss costs less than a decline; re-entry ping in their own area; handler/responder visibility.
  • Later: scores ease back to neutral; restore 90s or an "I'm looking" tap.
  • Decisions for Helen: own-area priority vs callout speed; cost of a miss vs a decline; 90s vs the tap.
So it doesn't happen again

The review-checklist skill

4.2 slipped through because commitments, scope and evidence were never checked in one place: Availability Confidence was marked Committed but never shipped, a fix was claimed in a text file but recorded nowhere, and "seasonal" was stated as fact. The skill runs the same six checks on any brief, every time.

The six checks

1
Owner
Person and role named. "The team" fails.
2
Success measure
Metric, baseline, target, read date.
3
Scope matches
What's promised at the start is what's asked at the end.
4
Problem before fix
Not a restated solution.
5
Evidence labels
Established vs hypothesis vs proposed-not-built.
6
The decision
What, who, by when.

Honest limits

What this report does not claim

Not established

  • Why the four's first pings were missed. A delivery failure was withdrawn as an idea; ticket wording says the ping arrived.
  • That the score ratchet is the cause. No per-responder scores exist in any log. A full acceptance gap is worth only ~19 travel minutes, which looks too small to explain the first-week loss on its own.
  • Whether 4.2's weights were deliberate, and whether scores reset on deploy (questions for Wen Li).
  • Seasonality: no prior-year data.
